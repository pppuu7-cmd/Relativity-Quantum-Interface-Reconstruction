## Abstract — v0.2

Laboratory tests at the gravity–quantum interface are often formulated either by selecting a candidate microscopic theory or by proposing a particular witness of gravitational nonclassicality. We develop a complementary operational reconstruction problem: **which components of quantum source information remain distinct after a declared finite calibration, before a microscopic interpretation of gravity is assigned?** Building on the established closed-time-path and stochastic-gravity distinction between one-point stress-energy, symmetrized fluctuations, and causal response, we organize the source information into an ordered hierarchy containing `J=<T>`, the symmetrized noise kernel `N`, the antisymmetric/commutator sector `D`, the retarded response `chi^R`, and higher CTP information. We then treat finite source calibration as a quotient on the physical state space. A finite-dimensional proposition shows that if the calibration map has a physical null direction on which a specified ordered-response functional is nonzero, then two positive source states can agree on every declared calibration observable while differing in that response. Constructive RQIR examples realize the required positivity, rank, calibration equality and detector-facing response simultaneously, and show that changing calibration geometry rotates the hidden direction. The result is an operational identifiability statement at the source level, not evidence that gravity is quantized and not a claim that gravity necessarily couples to `D` or `chi^R`. Classical, semiclassical, stochastic, measurement-feedback, postquantum and quantum-gravity descriptions can overlap in observable space and must be compared through the complete interface map. Paper I therefore supplies a calibration-aware source-information layer for the nuisance-profiled statistical analysis of Paper II.

## 1. Introduction

The empirical interface between gravitation and quantum theory is increasingly accessible to laboratory analysis. Proposed probes include matter-wave and spin interferometry, gravitationally mediated correlations and entanglement, quantum clocks, optomechanical tests, decoherence and diffusion measurements, field-disturbance tests, and observables designed to go beyond a static Newtonian-potential description. These programmes differ in experimental architecture and in what they aim to infer, but they share a basic inverse problem: the observed probability distribution is generated by a chain of source preparation, quantum matter dynamics, gravitational or effective interface dynamics, calibration, detector transfer, nuisance parameters and data reduction.

Consequently, a nonclassical-looking signal need not by itself determine a unique microscopic description of gravity. Conversely, microscopic models that are conceptually different may be operationally degenerate in a finite experimental domain. The appropriate inference target is therefore not automatically a theory label but an equivalence class of interface maps compatible with the declared observables and controls.

Relativity–Quantum Interface Reconstruction (RQIR) begins from this inverse perspective. Schematically,

`P_data(o|s) -> [I]`,

where `[I]` denotes an operational equivalence class of gravity–quantum interface maps after declared calibrations, baselines and nuisance freedoms are taken into account. RQIR does not begin by assuming that gravity is fundamentally classical, stochastic, quantum, emergent or hybrid.

Paper I isolates the earliest source-side layer of this programme. Its central question is deliberately narrower than “is gravity quantum?”:

> **What source information can remain operationally distinct after a declared finite calibration map?**

The analysis uses established quantum-field-theoretic and open-system structures. In particular, stochastic gravity and influence-functional methods already distinguish the stress-energy expectation value from its symmetrized fluctuation kernel and from response/dissipation structure. RQIR does **not** claim this separation as new. Instead, we use these objects as coordinates of an operational discrimination problem and ask what a finite calibration can and cannot identify.

This leads to a geometric statement. If a finite calibration map leaves a physical null direction and a specified ordered-response functional does not vanish on that direction, then two positive source states can be calibration-indistinguishable while remaining response-distinct. The proposition is elementary at the level of linear algebra; its purpose is to make the calibration quotient explicit in the gravity–quantum source problem and to connect it to constructive positive-state examples and detector-facing response.

The interpretation must remain conservative. Paper I does not assert that gravity couples to the matter commutator or retarded stress-energy response in nature. It does not assert that a nonzero matter commutator proves quantized geometry. It does not treat the observation of entanglement as an assumption-free proof of quantum gravity. Instead, it separates source-side information structure from the later questions of statistical identifiability, resource feasibility and model/comparator uniqueness.

### 1.1 Contributions

Within the frozen Paper-I scientific scope, the contribution is the following combined structure:

1. an **operational reconstruction framing** in which the inferred object is an interface equivalence class rather than a presupposed microscopic gravity theory;
2. an **ordered source-information hierarchy** using established `J`, `N`, `D`, `chi^R` and higher CTP objects as distinct operational coordinates unless a physical identity relates them;
3. a **finite calibration quotient** defined on physically admissible source-state perturbations;
4. a **calibration-nullspace proposition** showing when positive calibration-equivalent states can remain distinct under a specified ordered-response functional;
5. **constructive RQIR realizations** verifying positivity, rank, equality and response separation together;
6. a **calibration-steering relation** showing that the hidden direction depends on calibration geometry and is therefore a design variable;
7. an explicit **epistemic boundary** between source-side distinguishability and a claim about the ontology of gravity;
8. a controlled bridge from exact source separation to nuisance-profiled statistical identifiability in Paper II.

### 1.2 Non-claims

Paper I does **not** claim:

- empirical evidence for new physics;
- empirical evidence that gravity is quantized;
- that gravity necessarily transmits `D` or `chi^R`;
- that the mean/noise/response split is an RQIR discovery;
- that the generic fact of incomplete quantum tomography is new;
- that stochastic gravity is equivalent to a quantum theory of geometry;
- that entanglement alone is a sufficient witness under every admissible comparator class;
- relativistic completeness of the finite Toy009/Toy010 models;
- detector-level statistical identifiability after uncertain source preparation and nuisance profiling;
- apparatus-specific feasibility or wall-clock closure.

These exclusions define the scientific scope of the result.

## 2. Relation to established approaches

### 2.1 Semiclassical and stochastic gravity

Standard semiclassical gravity uses the renormalized stress-energy expectation value as the source of a classical metric, schematically

`G_mn + Lambda g_mn + H_mn^EFT = 8 pi G <T_mn>_ren`.

Stochastic gravity extends this mean-field description by including stress-energy fluctuations through the noise kernel and, in its influence-functional / closed-time-path formulation, contains both noise and response/dissipation structure [1]. This provides an important precedent for the ordered second-order hierarchy used below.

RQIR therefore treats mean-only semiclassical gravity as an early comparator and stochastic gravity as a stronger comparator. A proposed “fluctuation” signal cannot be interpreted as intrinsically quantum-gravitational merely because it lies beyond `<T>`.

### 2.2 Information-theoretic witnesses

The Bose–Marletto–Vedral family of proposals uses gravitationally mediated entanglement or related information-theoretic reasoning to test nonclassical gravitational dynamics [2–4]. More recently, LOCC-simulation inequalities have been proposed that can test gravitational nonclassicality without generating entanglement [5]. A 2025 Review of Modern Physics surveys this rapidly developing experimental-information-theoretic programme [6].

These works motivate a high-level operational viewpoint, but they do not eliminate the need to state the admitted comparator class and the assumptions that connect a measured witness to an ontological conclusion.

### 2.3 Classical, hybrid and postquantum alternatives

Consistent classical–quantum coupling remains an active area. Postquantum classical-gravity dynamics, irreversibility constraints on classical-gravity/quantum-matter couplings, stochastic decoherence/diffusion proposals, monitoring-and-feedback constructions and causal conditional semiclassical models all enlarge the space of alternatives that an experiment may need to exclude [7–11].

For RQIR this has a direct methodological consequence: a source-side information coordinate is not a model label. The same operational level can be populated by models with different microscopic interpretations.

### 2.4 Current debate over gravity-mediated entanglement

Aziz and Howl argued in 2025 that, with matter described in full QFT, local classical-gravity theories can generate entanglement through quantum matter propagation [12]. Subsequent work has challenged how relevant such matter-mediated channels are in realistic localized and bound macroscopic systems, and a 2026 analysis argued that particular collapse-based counterexamples violate locality assumptions required by entanglement-witness theorems [13,14].

Paper I does not attempt to settle this active debate. Its lesson is more general: the evidential meaning of a witness depends on the comparator class and on assumptions about locality, matter description, accessible channels and the complete observable map.

### 2.5 Beyond a static Newton potential

Chen and Giacomini identified quantum-gravity effects from delocalized sources that probe structure beyond the static Newton-potential regime [15]. This reinforces the value of an operational hierarchy broad enough to include dynamical response and field-sensitive observables rather than organizing the entire interface problem around one Newtonian witness.

## 3. Operational reconstruction of the gravity–quantum interface

Let `O={O_A}` denote operational observables. The index `A` includes source preparation, smearing or coarse graining, detector transfer, experimental setting and any renormalization prescription required for the observable to be meaningful.

For an explicit baseline `B` in a domain `D`, define

`Delta_A^(B)(D) = O_A^obs(D) - O_A^(B)(D)`.

Residuals defined relative to different baselines are not combined without an explicit baseline transformation.

Abstractly,

`I : (rho_matter, G, C, lambda) -> P(o|s)`,

where `G` denotes gravitational/interface degrees of freedom or effective variables, `C` denotes constraints and consistency structure, and `lambda` collects model and nuisance parameters.

The inverse problem is

`P_data(o|s) -> [I]`,

with `[I]` the equivalence class left after the declared operational quotient.

Paper I asks an earlier question: before assigning a microscopic interface, which source-side information coordinates are distinct, and which are eliminated by a finite calibration?

## 4. Ordered stress-energy information hierarchy

Define the centered source operator

`delta T_A(x) = T_A(x) - <T_A(x)>`.

### 4.1 One-point source

`J_A(x) = <T_A(x)>`.

This is the source coordinate retained by a mean-field semiclassical description.

### 4.2 Greater and lesser correlators

`G^>_AB(x,y) = <delta T_A(x) delta T_B(y)>`,

`G^<_AB(x,y) = <delta T_B(y) delta T_A(x)>`.

### 4.3 Symmetrized/noise sector

`N_AB(x,y) = (1/2)<{delta T_A(x),delta T_B(y)}>`

and therefore

`N = (G^> + G^<)/2`.

### 4.4 Antisymmetric/commutator sector

In the notation frozen in `docs/FOUNDATIONS.md`,

`D_AB(x,y) = (1/2i)<[delta T_A(x),delta T_B(y)]>`

so that

`G^> - G^< = 2 i D`.

**Notation caution.** Some stochastic-gravity literature also uses `D` for a dissipation kernel. In this manuscript `D` denotes the antisymmetric source commutator coordinate defined above; the physical retarded/dissipative response is represented explicitly by `chi^R` or described in words to avoid ambiguity.

### 4.5 Retarded response

With one declared sign convention,

`chi^R_AB(x,y) = (i/hbar) theta(x0-y0) <[T_A(x),T_B(y)]>`.

Thus the retarded source response is obtained from the commutator sector with causal support.

### 4.6 Ordering-preservation rule

The core algebraic rule is

> Matching `J` and `N` does not imply matching `D` or `chi^R` unless an additional physical identity, commutativity condition, spacelike-separation statement, equilibrium relation or controlled limiting argument supplies that implication.

This statement is compatible with established fluctuation-dissipation relations: such relations are additional physical structure valid in specified regimes, not a general algebraic identity between arbitrary calibrated states.

## 5. Closed-time-path parent object

A compact parent object for the ordered hierarchy is the Schwinger–Keldysh / closed-time-path generating functional

`Z_T[J_+,J_-] = Tr(U[J_+] rho_T U[J_-]^dagger)`,

with

`W_T = - i hbar ln Z_T`.

Functional derivatives generate branch-ordered correlators, including Wightman, time-ordered, retarded and higher nested-response structures. RQIR treats the appropriately smeared/renormalized equivalence class `[Z_T]` as a source-side information object and asks which projections survive an interface and detector map.

The use of a CTP source object is established field-theory technology and is not claimed as novel here. Its role is organizational: it prevents distinct operator orderings from being collapsed into a single undifferentiated covariance coordinate.

This source-side construction does not assume an operator-valued metric. It can be inserted into classical, stochastic, hybrid, postquantum or quantum gravitational/interface models.

## 6. Finite calibration as a quotient of source information

Let `V` be the real tangent space of Hermitian source-state perturbations after exact hard linear preparation constraints have been eliminated. Let

`A : V -> R^m`

be a finite calibration map. Its rows may represent matched means and other declared linear expectation constraints. If means are included among the equalities, selected symmetrized second-moment rows can also encode equality of the associated centered covariance entries.

Let

`c : V -> R`

be a specified linear ordered-response functional.

The operational question is geometric:

> Does the declared calibration remove every physical direction on which `c` changes?

If `ker A` is nontrivial, calibration leaves an equivalence class. If `c` vanishes on the entire nullspace, the response is fixed by that calibration. If there is a physical `n in ker A` with `c(n) != 0`, the response remains distinct inside the calibration equivalence class.

This is closely related to the established notion of informationally incomplete quantum measurements. The RQIR-specific step is to retain physical-state admissibility and test a designated ordered-response coordinate on the unresolved direction.

## 7. RQIR-THM-001 — finite calibration-nullspace response discriminant

### Proposition

Let `V` be the real tangent space of Hermitian source-state perturbations after all exact hard linear constraints in the declared preparation domain have been eliminated. Let

`A : V -> R^m`

be a finite linear calibration map with one-dimensional nullspace

`ker A = span{n}`,

and let

`c : V -> R`

be a linear response functional. Suppose:

1. `c(n) != 0`;
2. the nominal density operator `rho0` lies in the interior of the physical state set on the retained finite-dimensional Hilbert space;
3. `n` is represented by a Hermitian traceless perturbation compatible with the exact preparation constraints already imposed.

Then there exists `epsilon > 0` such that

`rho_+ = rho0 + epsilon n`,

`rho_- = rho0 - epsilon n`

are both physical states, satisfy identical declared calibration data,

`A(rho_+ - rho0) = A(rho_- - rho0) = 0`,

and have different response,

`c(rho_+ - rho_-) = 2 epsilon c(n) != 0`.

### Proof

Because `rho0` is strictly positive, choose

`0 < epsilon < lambda_min(rho0)/||n||_op`.

Weyl's eigenvalue bound guarantees that `rho0 +/- epsilon n` remain positive. Trace and every exact linear preparation constraint are preserved because `n` lies in the reduced tangent space. Since `A n = 0`, every declared finite calibration row agrees for the pair. Since `c(n) != 0`, their response differs by `2 epsilon c(n)`. QED.

### Scope and novelty of the proposition

The generic linear-algebra fact that incomplete measurements can have a nullspace is not new, and quantum tomography has an established theory of informational completeness. The purpose of RQIR-THM-001 is narrower: it makes explicit a positivity-preserving source-state pair inside a declared calibration quotient and couples that quotient to a specified ordered-response functional.

The proposition therefore supports the statement

> **finite calibration equality need not identify a designated ordered-response coordinate even within the physical state set**, 

not the broader claim that RQIR discovered measurement incompleteness.

The one-dimensional-nullspace form is retained because it is the cleanest closed Paper-I statement. Higher-dimensional generalizations follow by requiring at least one physical null vector not annihilated by `c`.

## 8. Constructive realization: Toy009 and Toy010

Toy009 and Toy010 are constructive finite models, not empirical models of relativistic matter. Their purpose is to verify that the nontrivial ingredients of the proposition coexist in one explicit physical-state realization.

The frozen numerical structure includes:

- calibration rank `24/25`;
- a one-dimensional hidden direction;
- positive physical state pairs;
- calibration-equality residuals at approximately `10^-15` numerical scale;
- equality of selected mean/noise coordinates;
- nonzero opposite ordered response;
- a detector-aware response coordinate surviving the declared finite calibration map.

These examples close three logical loopholes:

1. the null direction is compatible with positivity;
2. equality of the declared finite calibration rows does not force the chosen response to agree;
3. the response distinction survives the declared detector-facing projection rather than disappearing immediately under readout.

No empirical gravity claim is attached to the particular finite Hilbert-space dimension or coefficients.

## 9. Calibration geometry as an active design variable

Toy010 shows that calibration is not merely a passive verification stage. Changing the calibration geometry rotates the hidden direction.

For a smooth calibration map with normalized null vector `n`, the finite perturbative relation

`n' = - A^+ A' n`

implies the bound

`||n'|| <= ||A'|| / s_min(A)`

in the stated setting.

Thus an unchanged source Hamiltonian can support different unresolved state directions under different calibration designs, and consequently different values of `c(n)`. Source preparation, calibration and detector geometry should therefore be co-designed.

This is one of the aspects that distinguishes the RQIR use of a nullspace from merely noting that a measurement set is informationally incomplete: the orientation and conditioning of the quotient are treated as operational design variables.

## 10. Operational sensitivity hierarchy

The source-side analysis motivates the working hierarchy

- `L1 mean-sensitive`: only `J=<T>` is operationally relevant;
- `L2 noise-sensitive`: `N` contributes independent information;
- `L3 order/response-sensitive`: `D` or `chi^R` contributes independently;
- `L4 higher-order-sensitive`: higher CTP cumulants or nested responses are required;
- `L5 quantum-information-sensitive`: entanglement, channel or process observables provide additional constraints.

These levels classify **transmitted operational information**, not microscopic gravitational ontology.

A classical stochastic or measurement-feedback model can occupy a higher operational information level than a mean-only semiclassical model. Conversely, a quantized gravitational description need not produce a unique observable direction after all admitted comparators and nuisances are included.

The hierarchy is therefore a bookkeeping device for experimental discrimination, not a ladder from “classical” to “quantum gravity.”

## 11. Degeneracy discipline

### 11.1 Mean-field degeneracy

Agreement with `<T_mn>` tests only the mean-source coordinate. It does not establish sensitivity to stress-energy fluctuations or response.

### 11.2 Noise degeneracy

A fluctuation excess is not automatically an intrinsic gravitational quantum fluctuation. Stochastic gravity, matter-induced fluctuations, detector noise and environmental noise can populate overlapping fluctuation observables.

### 11.3 Response degeneracy

A nonzero matter commutator or retarded source response is a property of quantum matter and does not by itself imply an operator-valued metric. Any model claiming gravitational sensitivity to this sector must derive the corresponding observable map and compare it to classical causal-response, stochastic, hybrid and postquantum alternatives.

### 11.4 Quantum-information degeneracy

Entanglement and other nonclassical correlations can be powerful witnesses under specified assumptions, but the current literature shows that the admissible matter description, locality assumptions, hidden measurement channels and comparator set matter for interpretation [2–6,12–14]. Paper I therefore treats witness interpretation as a later model-comparison problem rather than building its core theorem on one witness.

### 11.5 Perturbative quantum gravity as comparator

Low-energy perturbative quantum gravity / gravitational EFT supplies controlled quantum-gravity predictions without choosing a UV completion. It is therefore a mandatory quantum comparator in later RQIR stages, not an automatic target theory and not evidence that a new theory is required.

## 12. Structural-identifiability bridge

Later RQIR comparator work exposed a distinct obstruction that can be stated briefly without reopening Paper I.

Exact on-shell information need not uniquely determine the off-shell/source-completed response object entering an operational interface map. Schematically,

`Gamma_B = Gamma_A + K2(p) H`,

with

`K2(p)=0`

on shell, allows the on-shell data to agree while an off-shell/source-completed derivative differs,

`D Gamma_B - D Gamma_A = K2(p) D H`.

For Paper I the lesson is structural: reduction to an on-shell or otherwise compressed description can quotient information needed by the source-completed observable map, just as finite calibration quotients state directions.

Detailed C5/source/Ward/IR calculations belong to Paper IV, not to the scientific core of Paper I.

## 13. From exact separation to statistical identifiability

Paper I asks whether a declared calibration leaves a response-sensitive physical direction:

`A n = 0`, `c(n) != 0`.

This exact statement does not determine whether a finite noisy experiment can infer the amplitude of that direction after source, apparatus and detector uncertainties are introduced.

Paper II asks whether the associated detector score survives nuisance profiling,

`F_beta|theta = F_bb - F_btheta F_thetatheta^{-1} F_thetab`,

or, in whitened geometry,

`F_beta|theta = ||(I-P_J) s_tilde||^2`.

Thus exact source-side distinguishability is necessary but not sufficient for experimental inferability.

This is the correct Paper-I endpoint: RQIR-NG-005 prevents an exact hidden-direction construction from being promoted directly to an experimental claim when its source amplitude is itself uncertain.

## 14. Discussion

The main role of Paper I is to move the gravity–quantum interface question one level upstream from a binary choice of microscopic ontology.

First, the source information is ordered. The distinction between mean, symmetrized fluctuation and causal response is established in quantum field theory and stochastic gravity, but it is frequently obscured when experimental proposals are reduced to a single coarse source statistic. RQIR retains the distinctions explicitly so that the interface, rather than the notation, decides which coordinates are transmitted.

Second, calibration defines an operational quotient. Equality of two preparations means equality under a declared finite map, not equality of the underlying states. RQIR-THM-001 packages this fact into a physical source-response statement: a positivity-compatible hidden direction can survive all declared calibration rows while carrying nonzero response.

Third, the quotient itself is designable. The hidden direction rotates as calibration geometry changes. Calibration therefore participates in discriminator design and should be optimized jointly with source preparation and detector response.

Fourth, operational nonclassicality and gravitational ontology are not identical questions. The recent literature includes entanglement witnesses, non-entanglement LOCC tests, stochastic and monitoring-feedback models, postquantum classical gravity, full-QFT matter channels and dynamical effects beyond a static Newton potential. Their coexistence makes comparator discipline unavoidable.

The RQIR series is designed around that separation of tasks. Paper I identifies exact source-information directions. Paper II tests statistical identifiability under nuisance profiling. Paper III converts surviving directions into physical resource and experiment-architecture requirements. Paper IV subjects existing gravity and quantum-gravity frameworks to a common comparator funnel. Paper V is conditional: a new Candidate Gravity construction is justified only if Paper IV leaves a robust comparator-subtracted residual and returns `NEW_REQUIRED`.

## 15. Conclusion

We formulated the first layer of Relativity–Quantum Interface Reconstruction as a calibration-aware source-information problem. Using established closed-time-path and stochastic-gravity structures, we retain the stress-energy expectation value, symmetrized noise, commutator/ordered sector, retarded response and higher ordered information as distinct operational coordinates unless a physical relation identifies them.

A finite calibration map defines an equivalence class on physically admissible source states. If that map leaves a physical null direction on which a specified ordered-response functional is nonzero, positive states exist that are calibration-equivalent but response-distinct. Constructive RQIR examples realize the required rank, positivity, equality and detector-facing response together. Calibration geometry can rotate the hidden direction, making calibration part of source-discriminator design rather than external bookkeeping.

The conclusion is intentionally limited. The result does not establish that gravity transmits the response coordinate, does not demonstrate quantized geometry and does not select a unique microscopic gravity model. It instead supplies a source-side operational structure that can be passed to the later statistical, resource and comparator stages of RQIR.
