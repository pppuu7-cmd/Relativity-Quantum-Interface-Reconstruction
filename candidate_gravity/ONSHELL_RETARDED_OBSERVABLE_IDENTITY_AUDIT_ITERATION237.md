## Decisive causal-response authority

The relevant modern distinction is between an in-out S-matrix amplitude and an asymptotic in-in observable.

Caron-Huot–Giroux–Hannesdottir–Mizera, JHEP 01 (2024) 139, arXiv:2308.02125, formulate asymptotic radiation observables as generalized-LSZ reductions of response functions and also as combinations of amplitudes and products of amplitudes. The time ordering is relaxed relative to an ordinary S-matrix element, and the in-in observable has distinct infrared structure.

Biswas–Parra-Martinez, JHEP 07 (2025) 037, derive classical asymptotic observables from amputated causal response functions in the Schwinger–Keldysh basis. Their construction makes the causal/retarded structure explicit and rederives KMOC-type observables from response functions rather than identifying them with a single in-out amplitude discontinuity.

Bini et al., Phys. Rev. D 109, 125008 (2024), give an especially direct waveform-level statement: the amplitude-based radiation observable contains a five-point amplitude term **plus a separate unitarity-cut term** (bilinear in lower amplitudes). The cut term is required in the physical observable and has frame/BMS significance.

Capatti–Zeng, Phys. Rev. D 111, 125002 (2025), further show equivalence of amplitude-based classical observables with worldline formalisms in tested sectors, with retarded causal flow emerging only after the appropriate combination of cut and uncut ingredients.

`D A_5^(1-loop)  ==  D Gamma3_ret,soft`

At the same perturbative order it generally requires additional cut/product-of-amplitudes terms generated by generalized LSZ / Schwinger–Keldysh ordering.

This is not repaired by the soft theorem alone: a soft factor acting on an in-out lower-point amplitude does not by itself reproduce the complete causal response, source/contact completion and cut-term bookkeeping.
