Dear Editors of Physical Review Research,

Please consider my manuscript, “Relativity–Quantum Interface Reconstruction II: Statistical Identifiability, Nuisance Geometry, and Detectability in Detector-Facing Likelihoods,” as a Regular Article in Physical Review Research.

The paper addresses a recurring inference problem in gravity–quantum interface experiments: an observable direction can survive an exact calibration quotient yet remain statistically degenerate with source, detector, or control nuisance parameters. I develop a covariance-aware tangent-space formulation that explicitly separates exact source/calibration survival, data-only local identifiability, externally assisted nuisance constraints, and finite-data sensitivity.

The main results are a nuisance-profiled projection/Schur geometry that remains valid for rank-deficient nuisance models; an exact variational formula with positive-semidefinite independent nuisance precision; nuisance-coordinate invariance and calibration-information monotonicity; an exposure no-go for exact score alignment; a shared-versus-branch-specific nuisance bound; and an exact two-band self-calibration law. The implementation is frozen by a seven-test deterministic reference certificate and independently stress-tested over 10,000 randomized problems including rank deficiency and correlated covariance. A minimal counterexample shows that threshold deletion of a weak but exactly collinear nuisance direction can manufacture a false unit-information signal.

The manuscript is deliberately conservative in interpretation. It does not claim new physics, global nonlinear identifiability, apparatus readiness, or that positive Fisher information implies practical detectability. Physical resource conversion is reserved for RQIR III.

The manuscript cites the current working version of RQIR I as an unpublished companion manuscript because RQIR II inherits its exact calibration-aware source-side interface. This Paper-II submission is intended to be assessed on its own statistical and reproducibility results. All code and numerical data needed for the tables and figures are publicly available in the project repository, and the manuscript includes both a Data Availability Statement and a substantive-AI-use disclosure consistent with APS policy.

Thank you for considering this work.
