\title{Nuisance-aware resource closure and traceable calibration for quantum sensing: Raman interferometry with a Ramsey-clock transfer benchmark}
\author{Aleksey Buyanov\\
\small Independent Researcher, Moscow, Russia\\
\small \href{mailto:pppuu7@gmail.com}{pppuu7@gmail.com}\\
\small ORCID: \href{https://orcid.org/0009-0001-2621-9305}{0009-0001-2621-9305}}
\date{11 September 2026}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Quantum-sensing forecasts often optimize raw sensitivity while treating nuisance parameters, calibration information, and experimental resources as separate afterthoughts. We formulate a nuisance-aware resource-closure criterion in which nonnegative allocations are optimized only after shared nuisance directions are profiled. For whitened target scores $\bm s_k$, nuisance score matrices $J_k$, and independent nuisance precision $\Lambda\succeq0$, the profiled information is the minimum over shared nuisance displacements of a resource-weighted residual-score norm plus the calibration penalty. The resulting information map is componentwise nondecreasing and concave in resource and positively homogeneous for data-only scaling, yielding convex maximum-information and minimum-budget problems and a nuisance-aware marginal information-per-cost rule. We first bind the method to a source-grounded three-pulse $^{87}$Rb Raman atom gravimeter using published cycle time, phase noise, detector noise, and vibration-spectrum information. A source-traceable colored covariance reproduces two published apparatus-level noise scales without fitted normalization. In the detector-facing probability likelihood, simultaneous profiling of phase, drift, contrast, readout gain, common scale, and finite-reference uncertainty leaves a modulated acceleration-like science amplitude locally estimable, whereas static science and uncalibrated-reference controls remain non-identifiable. A prospective differential Raman-chirp reference yields a conservative fractional reference uncertainty of $7.9563\times10^{-5}$ and a one-hour statistical forecast of $0.746604$ ng, with calibration/statistics crossover near $9.38\,\mu g$. To test cross-architecture transfer, we apply the identical frozen profiler to a reduced interleaved Ramsey/atomic-clock model with local-oscillator offset and drift nuisances: an unmodulated response gives zero profiled information, a signed four-setting control pattern restores unit normalized information, finite offset calibration follows $\mathcal I=\lambda/(1+\lambda)$, and an asymmetric allocation reduces the retained information to $0.84$. The result is a reusable experimental-design criterion for distinguishing resource shortage from nuisance-induced non-identifiability; it is neither a clock-performance forecast nor a claim of observed new physics.
\end{abstract}
