\begin{abstract}
Quantum-sensing forecasts often optimize raw sensitivity while treating nuisance parameters, calibration information, and experimental resources as separate afterthoughts. Relativity--Quantum Interface Reconstruction (RQIR) provides an ordered inference framework in which these layers are tested sequentially. Here we develop its resource-closure layer and apply it to Raman atom-interferometric gravimetry. For nonnegative allocations $e_k$, whitened target scores $\bm s_k$, nuisance score matrices $J_k$, and fixed independent nuisance precision $\Lambda\succeq0$, the profiled information is the minimum over shared nuisance displacements of the resource-weighted residual score norm plus the external calibration penalty. The resulting information map is componentwise nondecreasing and concave in resource, and positively homogeneous for data-only scaling, which yields convex maximum-information and minimum-budget problems and a nuisance-aware marginal information-per-cost rule. We bind this theory to a source-grounded three-pulse $^{87}$Rb Raman atom gravimeter using published cycle time, phase noise, detector noise, and vibration-spectrum information. A source-traceable colored covariance reproduces two independent published apparatus-level noise scales without fitted normalization. In the detector-facing probability likelihood, simultaneous profiling of phase, drift, contrast, readout gain, common scale, and finite-reference uncertainty leaves a modulated acceleration-like science amplitude locally estimable, while static science and uncalibrated-reference controls remain non-identifiable. A prospective differential Raman-chirp reference gives a conservative fractional reference uncertainty of $7.9563\times10^{-5}$ and a one-hour statistical forecast of $0.746604$ ng, with calibration/statistics crossover near $9.38\,\mu g$. The full computational chain is independently reproduced. The result is a general nuisance-aware resource-design method with an experimentally grounded quantum-sensing example, not a claim of an observed departure from established physics.
\end{abstract}
